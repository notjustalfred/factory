How to Trim a Video in Your Browser — Without Uploading It Anywhere

September 2, 2026

You recorded something on your phone, and now you just need to chop off the dead air at the start, cut out a bad take in the middle, and be done. That's it. You don't want to learn a timeline, sign up for anything, wait for a 400MB app to install, or watch your clip crawl up to some server and come back with a watermark stamped across it.

Here's how to do exactly that — trim, cut, delete, export — right in a browser tab, with nothing leaving your device.

The short version

  1. Open BigCut in any browser — phone or desktop.
  2. Open your video file from your device. It loads locally; nothing is uploaded.
  3. Set start and Set end to trim off the top and tail.
  4. Move the playhead to a bad spot, hit Cut here, select the piece, hit Delete.
  5. Export. You get the kept pieces back as a file, saved to your device.

No account, no email wall, no install. That's the whole flow.

Why "in the browser" beats a downloaded app for this

Most people who search how to trim a video don't want a video editor. They want scissors. Downloading CapCut, Clipchamp, or a desktop editor to shave three seconds off a clip is like buying a woodshop to open a letter.

A browser-based tool wins here because:

  • Nothing to install. It's a web page. Open the tab and go.
  • It works on the device the video is already on — including your phone, where most clips are recorded.
  • You skip the account. No "verify your email to download your file."

The catch with most online video cutters is that they aren't really in your browser — they upload your file to a server, process it there, and send it back (often watermarked, often behind an upload queue, sometimes behind a paywall for anything over 30 seconds). That's a privacy question and a waiting-in-line question you shouldn't have to think about for a simple trim.

"Without uploading it anywhere" — why that matters

BigCut runs 100% on your device. When you open a video, it stays in your browser; it never travels to a server. There's no cloud render, no upload progress bar, no queue. For anything personal — a family clip, a work recording, something you just don't want sitting on a stranger's server — that's the difference between "fine" and "no thanks."

It also means it's fast. There's no round trip. The editing happens where the file already lives.

The part nobody else fixes: buttons you can actually hit

Here's the real reason BigCut exists. The two things people do most in any editor — trimming and cutting — are usually buried under a hundred tiny buttons packed edge to edge. On a touchscreen especially, you go to cut a clip and fat-finger Import instead. The origin story of this tool is literally someone missing the cut button and hitting the wrong thing one too many times.

So BigCut has four oversized, well-spaced controls and nothing crowding them:

  • Set start / Set end — trim the whole clip's head and tail.
  • Cut here — split the clip at the playhead into pieces.
  • Delete — remove a piece you don't want.

Big hit targets, generous spacing, same layout on phone and desktop. You can't misfire it because there's almost nothing to misfire into. That's an accessibility and touch-first design a feature-maximalist editor can't bolt on later — the whole point is what's not there.

Be honest about what it is (and isn't)

BigCut is deliberately minimal. It does trim, cut, and delete. That's it. If you want transitions, effects, text overlays, audio mixing, or a multitrack timeline, this is not that tool — and it never pretends to be. That restraint is the feature.

Two honest specifics:

  • Export is WebM. Not MP4. WebM plays in browsers and most modern players and uploads fine to most platforms, but if you specifically need an MP4 for a picky destination, know that going in.
  • Export renders in real time. It plays through your kept pieces while recording the result, so a two-minute clip takes about two minutes to export. It's simple and it's honest about how it works.
