Turn an Image Into CSS Variables (and Tailwind, SCSS, or JSON)

September 17, 2026

You found the perfect palette in a photo — a client's product shot, a moodboard, a screenshot of a design you like. Now you need those colors in your code, not just on your screen. Most color tools stop at showing you HEX codes and leave the busywork to you: retyping six values into a :root block, formatting a Tailwind config by hand, hoping you didn't fat-finger a digit.

Huepine skips that. Drop an image in, get a palette, and export it as ready-to-paste CSS variables, SCSS, a Tailwind color config, or JSON with HEX/RGB/HSL. Here's how, and why it's built the way it is.

Extract the palette (free, in your browser)

Open the Huepine app and drop in any image — a JPG, PNG, or screenshot. Huepine runs a median-cut analysis on the pixels and pulls out the dominant colors, sorted by luminance so the palette reads from light to dark. Use the slider to choose anywhere from 3 to 10 colors depending on how rich you want the set.

The whole thing runs client-side. Your image never leaves your device — nothing is uploaded to a server. If you're pulling colors from unreleased product photos or NDA client work, that matters: the file stays on your machine.

Click any swatch to copy its HEX instantly. Click a swatch and Huepine also generates complementary, analogous, and triadic harmonies from that base color, so you can build out accents and states from a single dominant hue.

Export it as code

This is the part swatch-only pickers don't do. Once you've got a palette, pick an export format:

CSS variables

:root{
  --color-1: #1E2A38;
  --color-2: #3C6E9C;
  --color-3: #7FB2D9;
  --color-4: #C9DCE9;
}

Tailwind config — drop straight into theme.extend.colors:

colors: {
  brand1: '#1E2A38',
  brand2: '#3C6E9C',
  brand3: '#7FB2D9',
}

SCSS gives you $color-n variables, and JSON returns every color as HEX, RGB, and HSL — handy if you're feeding a design-token pipeline or a script. Copy the output and paste it into your project. No reformatting, no manual conversion.

What's free and what's paid — plainly

On-screen extraction, the harmonies, clicking to copy individual HEX codes, and a labeled PNG palette strip (great for a brief or a handoff deck) are all free. The code exports — CSS, SCSS, Tailwind, JSON — unlock with a one-time lifetime license. No subscription, no account, pay once and it's yours. We'd rather be upfront about that than bury it.

Why not just use Coolors or Adobe Color?

Both are good tools. But if your specific need is pulling a palette out of an image, they add friction:

  • Coolors puts its image picker behind Coolors Pro — a subscription. Getting colors out of a photo, the exact task above, is a paid feature.
  • Adobe Color requires an Adobe account to save or extract, and it's built around the Creative Cloud ecosystem.
  • Most free image pickers (imagecolorpicker.com and similar) will happily show you HEX/RGB/HSL, but stop there — you're still copying values one at a time and formatting the code yourself.

Huepine's bet is narrow on purpose: extraction is free and instant with no signup, nothing is uploaded, and the output is real code a developer can paste. That's the whole product.

Honest limits

Huepine does one image at a time. There's no batch mode, no cloud sync, and no saved palette library or account — it's deliberately fast and local. If your workflow depends on storing hundreds of palettes in an online account, a heavier tool is a better fit. If you just need to get this image's colors into this project's code, that's exactly what it's for.
